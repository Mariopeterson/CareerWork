An Ancient Land, a Modern Heartbeat

South Korea sits on a mountainous peninsula in East Asia, home to roughly 51 million people and one of the world's most dynamic cultures. In a single day you can wander a 600-year-old palace in the morning, ride a high-speed train through terraced rice fields by afternoon, and end the night in a glowing district of cafés, markets, and music. Four distinct seasons shape the rhythm of life here, from cherry-blossom springs to snow-dusted winters.

Culture & Heritage

Korean culture blends deep tradition with a global creative wave. The Korean alphabet, Hangeul, was created in 1443 by King Sejong the Great to make reading and writing available to everyone, and it is still celebrated as one of the most logical writing systems ever designed.

Visitors can walk the grounds of Seoul's five grand palaces, led by the majestic Gyeongbokgung, or slip on a flowing hanbok for the day. Meanwhile, K-pop, K-dramas, and award-winning films like Parasite have carried Korean storytelling to every corner of the world.

A Feast for the Senses

Korean food is built for sharing. A single meal arrives surrounded by banchan, small side dishes that change with the season, anchored by staples like kimchi, the country's beloved fermented cabbage. Try a sizzling plate of bulgogi or samgyeopsal at a Korean BBQ table, a colorful bowl of bibimbap, or a paper cup of spicy tteokbokki from a bustling street stall. Every bite is a mix of heat, tang, and comfort.

The capital region: palaces, markets, and the pulse of the nation.

The tranquil heartland of hot springs and heritage towns.

The soul of Korean cuisine and the storytelling song of pansori.

Ancient Silla capital Gyeongju and the seaside city of Busan.

Jeju Island

A volcanic isle of waterfalls, beaches, and haenyeo sea divers.

When to Go

Getting Around

The KTX high-speed rail links major cities in a flash, and a single rechargeable T-money card covers subways, buses, and even taxis.

Local Etiquette

Bow slightly to greet, remove your shoes indoors, and offer or receive items with two hands. Small gestures go a long way here.
